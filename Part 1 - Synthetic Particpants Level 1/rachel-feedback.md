# Rachel's Feedback on SecureHorizon Webinar Email

## 1. First impression of the subject line — would I open it?

The subject line is **"The Future of IT Resilience"** and honestly, it's borderline. "The Future of..." is a format I've seen a hundred times — it reads like a keynote title, not something that tells me what I'll actually *get*. On my morning train, scanning on my phone, it wouldn't stand out from the other vendor noise in my inbox.

I'd give it a **50/50 chance** of being opened. What *might* save it is the sender name — if I'd heard of SecureHorizon before, maybe. But "The Future of IT Resilience" sounds like it could be anything from a whitepaper pitch to a product launch dressed up as thought leadership.

A subject line like *"Cut your MTTR by 60% — here's the framework"* would stop my thumb.

## 2. If I opened it, what catches my attention first?

The **$145,000 per incident** stat. That's the kind of number I can use in a conversation with my CTO when I'm justifying budget or headcount. It's specific enough to feel credible, and it's framed around *my* world — mid-market, downtime costs.

Second thing I'd notice: **Dr. Anya Patel, Former Global Head of IT Operations, Westpac.** That's a real practitioner credential. If she's actually sharing war stories and not just lending her name, that's compelling. The SecureHorizon CTO being the other speaker does make me a little wary — that's where the product pitch usually hides.

## 3. Would I register?

**Leaning yes, but with reservations.** Here's what's working for me:

- The topic list is practical, not abstract — incident response automation, a self-assessment framework, predictive monitoring. These are things I'm actually dealing with.
- "Who this is for" section describes *me* almost exactly — IT Ops Manager, mid-market, 200-2,000 employees.
- The disclaimer at the bottom saying *"This isn't a product demo"* tells me they know I'm skeptical, which ironically makes me slightly less skeptical.
- The recording + template offer lowers my commitment — if it turns out to be rubbish, I've only lost a click.

What gives me pause: **"A live masterclass for IT leaders"** is the kind of phrasing that makes my eyes roll. "Masterclass" feels like marketing inflation. And "AI-powered monitoring" as a topic — I'd want to know if that's genuinely vendor-neutral or just a segue into their product.

## 4. What would need to change?

To move me from *leaning yes* to *confident yes*:

- **Drop "masterclass"** — call it a "live session" or just "webinar." Don't oversell.
- **Name a specific incident or case study** in the email body. Something like *"How [Company X] reduced their recovery time from 4 hours to 40 minutes after a ransomware event."* Show me you have real stories, not just frameworks.
- **Clarify the AI topic** — is this tool-agnostic or a SecureHorizon product walkthrough? If it's tool-agnostic, say so explicitly.
- **Personalise the greeting** — "Hi there" on what's supposed to be a professional invitation feels lazy. You know my name; use it.
- **Add a time commitment** — how long is this? 30 minutes? 60? 90? I don't see a duration anywhere. That matters when I'm blocking out my calendar.

---

*Overall: this is a well-constructed email — better than 80% of the vendor invites I receive. The design is clean, the targeting is right, and the content topics are relevant. It just needs a sharper subject line, a bit less marketing polish, and a concrete case study to seal the deal.*
